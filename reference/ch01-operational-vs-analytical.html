Operational vs Analytical

Operational systems run the product. Analytical systems study what the product did. Tell them apart by how they touch data.

The one-question test: does it touch a few records found by key (operational), or scan many records to summarise them (analytical)?

The differences (DDIA Table 1-1)

Operational (OLTP)Analytical (OLAP)
Main readsPoint queries: one record by keyAggregate over huge numbers of records
Main writesCreate, update, delete single recordsBulk import (ETL) or event stream
Human userEnd user of the appInternal analyst, for decisions
Machine userIs this action allowed?Fraud and abuse detection
QueriesFixed set, written into the appAd hoc: whatever the analyst asks
Data showsLatest state, nowHistory of events over time
SizeGB to TBTB to PB

Who uses what

  • Backend engineers build operational services that read and write data for users.
  • Business analysts write reports for management (business intelligence).
  • Data scientists find patterns and build features like recommendations.
  • Data engineers connect the operational and analytical sides.

Blurry cases

  • HTAP databases try to do both. Often two engines behind one interface. They don't replace a warehouse.
  • Product / real-time analytics: analytical queries shown to end users (e.g. a creator's views chart). Systems: Pinot, Druid, ClickHouse.

Why analytics gets its own store

  1. Silos. A report needs data from many operational systems.
  2. Wrong shape. Operational schemas suit point lookups, not scans.
  3. Cost. A big scan slows the app for every user.
  4. Access. Security and compliance may forbid direct queries.

The answer since the late 1980s: a separate data warehouse, filled by ETL. (Details in level 1.2.)

Say it in an interview

"User-facing reads and writes go to the operational database: point lookups by key, low latency. The dashboards are an analytical workload, so I'd copy the data into a warehouse and run them there. That way a big report can never slow down checkout."

Source: Kleppmann & Riccomini, DDIA 2nd ed., Ch 1, "Operational Versus Analytical Systems" and "Data Warehousing" (1st ed. Ch 3, "Transaction Processing or Analytics?").
