# Mission: Designing Data-Intensive Systems (via DDIA)

## Why
Make sound, defensible architecture decisions about the data systems I build and run at work, and be able to show that same judgment in senior-level system-design interviews.

## Success looks like
- Given a feature at work, I can name the data-system trade-offs involved (storage engine, replication, sharding, isolation level, batch vs stream) and justify a choice in a design doc.
- I can diagnose real incidents (replication lag anomalies, lost updates, hot shards, tail-latency spikes) using DDIA's vocabulary and mechanisms.
- I can run a 45-minute system-design interview end-to-end: clarify requirements, estimate load, sketch an architecture, and defend trade-offs under follow-up questions.
- I have worked through every chapter of *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* (2nd ed.) and can recall its core ideas weeks later, not just right after reading.

## Constraints
- Learning should be gamified: interactive browser lessons with XP, ranks, streaks and boss fights (see [NOTES.md](NOTES.md)).
- Primary text is DDIA 2nd edition (Kleppmann & Riccomini, O'Reilly, 2026).
- Pace and weekly time budget: _not yet stated — ask._

## Out of scope
- Implementing a database or consensus algorithm from scratch.
- Vendor certification material (AWS/GCP exams) and product-specific tuning beyond what illustrates a DDIA idea.
